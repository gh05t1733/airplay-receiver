## CRITICAL: protocol reality check (verify with web research, do NOT assume the vault spec is right)
The vault spec describes the PC as an "RTSP receiver" that *browses* mDNS to *find* the
MacBook. For AirPlay screen mirroring the direction is the opposite: the PC must act as an
AirPlay **receiver/server** that *advertises* itself over mDNS/DNS-SD and the MacBook (the
sender) discovers and connects to it. Research and resolve, with sources:
- which mDNS services must be registered/advertised by the PC (`_airplay._tcp.local`,
  `_raop._tcp.local`, TXT record keys: deviceid, features, flags, pk, pi, srcvers, vv, model,
  rsf, ft, am, sf, md) — and whether browsing is needed at all;
- the real RTSP handshake for mirroring: OPTIONS → ANNOUNCE → SETUP (streams) →
  RECORD → (GET_PARAMETER keepalive), plus `SETUP` over TCP vs UDP interleaved, and the
  `SET_PARAMETER` progress/heartbeat;
- pairing/encryption reality: legacy (no auth), PIN pairing, and the newer FairPlay/SRP
  pairing + AES-128-CBC/HMAC-SHA1 stream decryption — what is *required* for macOS Sonoma/Sequoia
  (AirPlay 2) vs what can be deferred;
- what is legally/technically safe to reuse: study UxPlay (RPiPlay successor),
  shairport-sync (audio), AirPlayServer/pyatv (protocol reference), GnuGPL vs MIT/LGPL
  implications if we link GPL code — the project should be Apache-2.0/MIT, so flag any
  licence conflict clearly.
State plainly what Sprint 1 can realistically achieve (e.g. "PC advertises as AirPlay
receiver, Mac sees it in Control Center as a screen-mirroring target") vs what needs the
encryption/pairing work in Sprint 2.
